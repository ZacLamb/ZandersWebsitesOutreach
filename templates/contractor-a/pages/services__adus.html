Overview

ADUs & In-Law Apartments in [[CITY]] & the [[CITY]] Area

An ADU (accessory dwelling unit) is a second, smaller home on your property — a backyard cottage, an apartment over the garage, or an in-law suite in the basement or an addition. It’s a place for Mom or Dad, a grown kid who needs a start, or rental income that helps pay the mortgage. We build them from the ground up: foundation, framing, utilities, finishes and the permits in between.

We start with a site visit to look at your lot, your septic or sewer, and where utilities can run. Then we work out the layout and a written price, handle the permits, and build it like it’s our own — with one crew and one guy to call from the first shovel to the final walkthrough.

What we do

  • Detached backyard cottages
  • Garage conversions and apartments over the garage
  • In-law suites in additions and basements
  • Foundations, framing, roofing and siding
  • Kitchens, baths, flooring and finish work
  • Permits and coordination with your town

Who builds an ADU?

One lot. Two homes. A lot of options.

  • Aging parents — close enough to help, with their own front door
  • Grown kids — a real first place without the Boston-area rent
  • Rental income — a long-term tenant helps cover the mortgage
  • Downsizing — move into the ADU and rent or hand down the main house

Rules vary by town (setbacks, height, septic and short-term rentals), so we check yours before anything gets drawn up. Source: Mass.gov ADU guidance.

Common questions

ADUs & In-Law Apartments FAQ

Can I build an ADU in Massachusetts without a special permit?

In most cases, yes. Since early 2025, the state’s Affordable Homes Act lets homeowners in single-family zones build one ADU by right — no special permit. Towns can still apply things like setbacks, height limits, site plan review and Title 5 septic rules, so we check your town’s rules before anything gets drawn up.

How big can an ADU be?

Under the state law, a by-right ADU can be up to 900 square feet or half the size of your main house, whichever is smaller. Some towns allow bigger ones with extra approval.

Do I have to live on the property or rent it to family?

No. Massachusetts towns can’t require owner-occupancy or a family relationship for a by-right ADU, so you can rent it out long-term. Some towns do restrict short-term rentals like Airbnb.

Do you build ADUs in Rhode Island too?

Yes. Rhode Island has its own ADU rules, and they vary by town. We’ll check what applies at your address before we price the job.

What does an ADU cost?

It depends on the size, whether it’s detached or a conversion, and what your site needs for foundation and utilities. We’ll come out, look at your property and give you a straight, written number.
